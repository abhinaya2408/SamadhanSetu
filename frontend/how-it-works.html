The full process

From a citizen's description to a verified solution

Each stage below states exactly what the platform does, what the AI does, and who decides.

  1. 1

    A citizen reports a problem

    The problem is stored exactly as written. The citizen's own wording is preserved and never rewritten by AI.

  2. 2

    AI screening produces a recommendation

    Groq (llama-3.3-70b-versatile) — or a local rules engine when Groq is not configured — summarises the problem, suggests a category and skills, lists missing information and clarification questions, and flags whether the outcome depends on government action. Its output is labelled a recommendation only.

  3. 3

    A human reviewer decides

    A faculty, university-admin or platform-admin reviewer records one of: approve for student innovation, request clarification, exclude as government-dependent, or decline. Every decision requires a written reason and is written to a permanent audit trail.

  4. 4

    A university forms a team

    Approved problems can be accepted by a university. Student matching is a transparent, deterministic weighted score (skills 40%, experience 20%, interest 15%, availability 15%, diversity 10%) with a written reason for every student suggested. A mentor approves the team.

  5. 5

    The team builds and reports

    Tasks, milestones, evidence and weekly reports are tracked. Every member accepts a Responsibility Form before work is assigned. Mentors review milestones with written comments.

  6. 6

    Impact is recorded honestly

    Impact reports separate what was proven from what is still pending, and record limitations. Only university staff can mark a report verified, and only reports that state their own limitations reach the public impact page.

Honesty

What this build does not do

  • —No web-wide search. Prior-solution matching reads only this deployment's internal repository. It does not search GitHub, papers or the open web, and the interface says so where results are shown.
  • —No unsupervised AI decisions. AI never approves a problem, assigns a student, scores a submission, or verifies impact.
  • —Model confidence is self-reported. Any confidence number comes from the model itself and is not a calibrated probability; it is labelled as such.
  • —Progress and innovation are separate. Competition rankings never merge execution progress with human judge scores into one misleading number.
  • —Demo data is labelled. Seeded demonstration records carry a visible Demo data tag.

Privacy & access

  • ✓Companies see students only when the student opted in to portfolio sharing. Direct contact details are never disclosed.
  • ✓Students see only projects they are active members of; the backend enforces this, not just the interface.
  • ✓Sessions use httpOnly cookies with a CSRF-protecting request header. Passwords are stored only as salted PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hashes.
  • ✓Platform administrator accounts are provisioned by operators and cannot be created through public registration.
